Overall this year had, in terms of quantity, less to offer than most years? There is a market recession for publishing intelligent non-fiction, though I am not sure whether that is the relevant cause here, given how niche my interests can be.

Of all those, my number one pick would be Anna Arno, Paul Celan: A Life, by far the best book on one of our greatest poets, and a look at how personal lives operate as well. Here Henry Oliver covers the book (and others as well):

What a splendid, splendid book. Adorno said that “To write poetry after Auschwitz is barbaric.” Celan proved him wrong. Naturally, this is a difficult subject matter to read about: not the sort of book you ought to read if you are sitting up alone at night. Arno has everything a biographer ought to have: mastery of the details, sensitive feeling for the poetry, and a calm controlled narrative style, so that the horrors and traumas can be dealt with properly, as can the ordinary aspects of life. This will be (one of) the best work(s) of literary non-fiction published this year. I don’t find Celan easy to apprehend in translation and I learned a lot from this book.

Recommended. And here are previous installments in this series, I do one each year. I like it to appear right before the peak of shopping season, and will write an addendum for what comes out between now and year’s end.
