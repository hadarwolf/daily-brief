If you’ve ever struggled to stop doomscrolling before bed, impulse buying on your credit card or eating one too many sweets, you’ve probably noticed that simply knowing something is harmful doesn’t always translate into taking better actions. But as the US psychiatrist and educator Tracey Marks explains in this short video, we can leverage how our brains are wired to close that gap and break a bad habit. Marks starts by debunking common and unhelpful myths about what it takes to make a change – chief among them the idea that willpower alone is enough – before moving on to concrete strategies, like making the things that trigger a bad habit harder to access, and to how reframing our sense of identity can make new habits stick.
