Across the centuries, people have reported seeing familiar shapes – above all, faces – in unlikely places, from clouds and marble to slices of toast bearing the image of Jesus. In this video, Mary McGillivray, a digital creator and PhD student at the University of Melbourne, explores this phenomenon, known as pareidolia. Drawing on conversations with a geologist and a vision scientist, McGillivray asks how such images arise, both in the patterns of stone and in the workings of the brain. Highlighting examples of figures and faces hidden in Renaissance art, she also reflects on what these images have meant to us over time, and what our tendency to see them might reveal about our capacity for creativity.
