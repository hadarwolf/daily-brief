In Fort Worth, Texas, a massive, dilapidated building serves as an ever-present symbol of racial violence and segregation to those who know its history. Constructed in the 1920s, the structure, initially known as Klavern No 101, was briefly the auditorium and headquarters of Fort Worth’s Ku Klux Klan chapter. It stood between downtown and the North Side, where migrants and communities of colour had settled, so many residents had to pass it on their way to work. In this short documentary, the director Chris Neighbors, a member of the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma, brings together scholars, community members and those directly involved in redesigning the space to reflect on its past and the thinking behind its transformation into an arts and community centre. Filmed inside the building itself, their conversations consider a question that extends far beyond Fort Worth: how do we preserve difficult histories without preserving the values they once stood for?
