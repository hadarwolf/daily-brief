The Argentine physicist Gabriel Mindlin has spent much of his career researching how birds learn to sing. He focuses on the rufous-collared sparrow (Zonotrichia capensis), a species common across urban and suburban Argentina, where it’s often called a ‘chingolo’. As in other songbirds, the male’s song is not innate but passed down from adult males to younger ones, who sing it throughout their lives to guard territory and find a mate.

In this short documentary, the Argentine director Martín Benchimol chronicles Mindlin’s latest breakthrough. Comparing the songs of today’s chingolos with those transcribed by hand in the 1960s, Mindlin realised that some melodies had survived the decades while others had disappeared. Using mathematical models, he and his team set out to revive the vanished songs and teach them back to living birds. With a cinematic eye, Benchimol follows the researchers as they attempt an audacious act of cultural engineering, building robotic song tutors, planting them in the wild and tracking whether young birds pick up these lost tunes.
