In general I am bullish on the commercial prospects of AI companies, including Anthropic (do note I am on the Advisory Board, though with zero governance role and zero access to internal information).

But I view today’s IPO as a sign of bearishness on any kind of extreme superintelligence, no matter what involved parties might say.

If superintelligence is coming soon, why turn yourself into a public company, with all the extra scrutiny and regulation that requires? Raising extra money through debt is the obvious alternative — why cut other in on such an amazing deal?

Furthermore, true superintelligence is likely to raise real interest rates, which raises the return to borrowing today (you can always reinvest at higher rates later, if you do not need the money for compute, etc.).

Now raising money through debt would be relatively costly for Anthropic, but at the very least they could have shrunk the size of the IPO and borrowed more at the margin. Claude says they would have had to pay about ten to eleven percent on that debt. Alternately, they could just take on Jane Street, Citadel, and others and try to earn money through direct commercial activities (but in fact they cannot, note that).

So presumably Anthropic thinks that pending advances, whatever you might wish to call them, will not with any great certainty yield ten to eleven percent at the margin. That is a perfectly fine view for them to hold, but you all should use the implied inference to calm down a bit about what is coming.

I myself am taking that wise and prudent financial decision under advisement, and it does not imply an incredibly radical near future. Keep in mind that the average return on American corporate capital is about seven percent.

Addendum: Maybe there is some clever 3-D chess strategy whereby they do the IPO, and then turn around and borrow lots at a much lower rate, say six to seven percent. And still come out ahead, relative to no IPO, by busting through a credit constraint. Maybe. We’ll see if that happens, but it strikes me as a rather strained possibility.
