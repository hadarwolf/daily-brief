Recently many people are debating whether AI will lead to our disempowerment. One simple example is that we might use AI to pursue or solve various intellectual tasks for us, rather than doing it ourselves.

So say there are twenty important math problems, and we let the AIs (with some human guidance) take a crack at them. Say the AIs solve seventeen of those problems, the humans one, and two remain unsolved.

Alternately, say we ban or limit AIs, and over a longer period of time the humans solve three of those problems. The rest go unsolved.

Arguably in the latter situation the humans are exercising more agency and, as unaided humans, achieving greater things. But in the former situation there is more discovery, possibly more usefulness, and the marginal impact of total human effort (including building the AIs) is far greater.

Which scenario involves greater “disempowerment”? I do not view the concept of disempowerment as entirely well defined, but it seems to my virgin eyes that the humans are more “disempowered” in the latter case, where only three of the problems get solved.

And take those two extra problems that get solved (eventually) by humans. Should we feel empowered if we achieve two theorem proofs due to what you might call protectionism, or how about labeling it “DEI for humans”?

Say I was working on a difficult economics problem, and I learned that an unpublished manuscript from China had solved the problem. But no one else knows about the manuscript, except for me that is. I could announce the manuscript and give it full credit. Or I could ignore the manuscript and claim the credit, and the “empowerment,” for myself. Should I feel so satisfied by that latter approach?

Or let’s say that Chinese manuscript does not exist, but I could help to create it by a well-targeted EV grant. I decline to give the grant so I can do the work myself and feel “empowered.” Am I really so empowered? Or is it a kind of phony triumph, because someone or something else could have done it better.

(One thing I have always liked about being an academic economist, or for that matter an internet writer, is knowing that I am competing against the entire world. In the U.S. academic job market there has been de facto open borders for a long time, since university employers usually will get visas for their hires, and do not shy away from doing that, even today.)

As for AI, should I feel so good and empowered if I achieve things only by deliberately holding the machines back? Or, in that scenario, am I just a loser who deliberately shuns competition?

In general, I am not persuaded by the notion of empowering humans by bathing the world in protectionism and “DEI for humans.” I feel humans will be empowered best by finding their true marginally most important contributions and making those. Whether or not that involves doing the exact same things we are contributing at today.
